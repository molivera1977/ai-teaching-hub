Step 1 of 6 — What is AI?

👋 Welcome! What IS AI?

You've probably seen and heard the term AI everywhere lately — on the news, on TV, maybe even from fellow teachers at lunch. But what do those two letters even mean?

Artificial Intelligence

A computer program that learned from a huge amount of writing. You ask it something in plain English, and it writes back in seconds. It knows a lot and never gets tired — but it can sound sure of itself and still be wrong, so you stay the teacher and check its work.

"AI in the teaching world is like having a teaching assistant who's available any time of day, willing to help with every part of your job, and never runs out of energy."

With something as simple as writing a text, a teacher can use AI to...

📅 Write me a lesson plan 📚 Build a 2-week unit 📝 Create a quiz 🎯 Make a rubric 💡 Give me 10 lesson ideas ✉️ Write a parent letter ✏️ Write a sub plan 📖 Summarize this article for my students 🗂️ Create a word search 🎓 Explain this concept in simpler terms

Step 2 of 6 — Why Teachers Love AI

🌟 Why do teachers who use AI love it?

Here's the honest truth: teachers don't need AI to be great at their job. But once they try it, most never want to go back. Here's why.

📅

Full lesson plans in minutes

Tell AI your topic, your grade level, and how long the lesson should be — and it writes you a complete lesson plan with an objective, activities, and a closing. What used to take an hour takes 5 minutes.

📚

Entire units of study, built to order

Tell AI: "Build me a 2-week unit on the American Revolution for 4th grade" — and it drafts a day-by-day plan with activities and objectives. You can even say how many days per week, what subjects to cover, and what standards to hit. Then you check it and make it yours.

🎯

Different versions for different students

Need the same reading passage at three different reading levels? AI does all three at once. Every student gets something that fits them — without you rewriting everything by hand.

📝

Quizzes, rubrics, and worksheets on demand

Type what you need and AI builds it. A 10-question quiz on fractions, a rubric for a science project, a writing prompt for Monday morning — ready in seconds.

💡

A brainstorming partner that never gets tired

Stuck on how to introduce a tricky concept? Ask AI for 10 ideas. Hate them all? Ask for 10 more. It never runs out of suggestions and never minds being told to try again.

🔧

You're always the one in charge

AI gives you a starting point — you decide what stays, what changes, and what gets tossed. Nothing goes to students without your eyes on it first.

🧠

Tell it how you like things — and it follows along

As you work with AI, you can tell it exactly how you like things done. Prefer your quizzes in a certain format? Want your reading passages to always have a vocabulary section at the bottom? Just tell it, and it will follow that for the rest of the chat. Some tools can also remember you from one chat to the next (a setting called Memory). It's optional — you can try it later. Either way, keep student names out. The more you share your preferences, the more it feels like working with someone who already knows your style.

…and these are just a handful of the hundreds of reasons teachers fall in love with AI once they give it a try.

⚠️ Three things to know right away

1. AI can get things wrong. It can make up facts, misspell names, or give you something that just doesn't quite fit.

👀

Always read what it gives you before sharing it with students — think of it like proofreading a lesson plan from a substitute.

💡

Here's a trick: you can ask AI to double-check the very thing it just gave you. It won't get offended. Try:

"Read that back and check it for any factual errors or awkward phrasing."
🔒

2. Keep your students private. Never type a student's name, ID number, grades, IEP or 504 details, or behavior notes into AI. Write "a student who…" instead — you can add the name yourself later.

🏫

3. Follow your school's rules. Use the AI tools your school allows, and ask your technology person if you're not sure. These tools are for you, the teacher — students shouldn't sign up for them on their own.

Step 5 of 6 — Your First Step

🚀 Your First Step

4 simple steps. Takes about 2 minutes. No downloads. No credit card.

1
🌐 Open your web browser
You're already doing this step — it's the app you're using right now to read this page. Chrome, Safari, and Edge are the most common ones.
2
🔗 Go to the website
Left-click the address bar — the long white bar at the top of your browser — type one of these addresses, and press Enter. (On a phone or tablet, tap it instead; it may be at the bottom of the screen.) Or just left-click the logo of your choice below and the link will take you there.
claude.ai/teachers FREE for teachers chatgpt.com/plans/k12-teachers FREE for teachers gemini.google.com copilot.microsoft.com
3
📝 Create a free account
Left-click Sign Up or Get Started and enter your email. Using Claude? Sign up with your school email — its free teacher plan doesn't accept personal addresses like Gmail. (For the other tools, not sure whether to use your school or personal email? Ask your school's technology person — some schools have a rule.) Many sites also offer shortcuts so you don't have to create a new password at all:
"Sign in with Google" — uses your existing Google account. No new password needed.
"Sign in with Apple" — uses your existing Apple ID. Works great on iPhones and Macs.
For Claude, skip these shortcuts unless "Sign in with Google" uses your school account (not sure? Just type your school email). For the other tools, any of them work — use whichever feels easiest.
4
💬 Say hello
Left-click inside the typing box at the bottom of the screen, type anything — even just Hello — and press Enter. That's it. You're using AI. 🎉

🔓 When Free Starts Feeling Limiting

Free plans are a great way to get started — and for many tasks, they're enough. Here's what each free plan limits, and what changes if you upgrade.

Claude 🎉 FREE for U.S. K-12 Teachers

Free limits: Your use resets every 5 hours and every week. Long conversations use it up faster. (Word and PDF downloads are included on the free plan.)

⭐ Claude for Teachers — free for verified U.S. K-12 teachers through July 30, 2028 (everyone else: Pro, $20/month) — unlocks:

  • More use than the free plan — so you run out less often in a busy week
  • More Claude models (different versions of Claude — you don't have to pick one)
  • More Projects — keep your class setup, grade level, and teaching style in one place (the free plan allows up to 5)
  • Claude Code — builds your classroom website and can put it online for you (Lesson 6 bonus)
  • Teaching skills — ready-made help for common teacher tasks (teacher plan)

How to get it: Sign up with your school email at claude.ai/teachers.

ChatGPT 🎉 FREE for U.S. K-12 Teachers

Regular free plan: Unlimited text chats, but limited file uploads, image creation, and voice chats.

⭐ ChatGPT for Teachers — completely free through June 2028:

  • Unlimited use of ChatGPT's best model
  • Image creation and file uploads
  • Voice mode
  • Connections to apps you already use

How to get it: Get verified as a teacher at chatgpt.com/plans/k12-teachers.

Google Gemini Often free at school

Free limits: Limits that reset every 5 hours (with a weekly cap). In Gmail, only basic help (Help Me Write and Suggested Replies), and no Gemini inside Google Docs.

Google AI Pro ($19.99/mo) adds about 4× more Gemini use (including more Deep Research) and Gemini in Gmail and Docs. Your school's upgraded Google plan (Education Plus or an add-on) adds Gemini inside Docs, Slides, and Sheets. Ask your tech person if your school already has it.

Microsoft Copilot Often free at school

Free limits: Works as a standalone chat tool (and with a school Microsoft account, a basic Copilot in Outlook). Copilot inside Word, Excel, and PowerPoint needs the Microsoft 365 Copilot add-on your school buys.

Microsoft 365 Copilot adds: AI directly inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Teams, and Outlook. Draft a lesson plan in Word, summarize a parent email in Outlook, or turn bullet points into a full presentation. Schools buy this as an add-on ($18 per person a month) — worth asking your tech person if yours has it.

Canva Free for teachers

Free limits: Paid templates and premium elements are locked, only a little AI (including making pictures), limited storage.

Canva for Education (free with school verification): Unlocks most of Canva Pro — AI image generation, background remover, thousands of premium templates, and the ability to assign Canva projects directly to students. Completely free for verified teachers.

Perplexity $10/mo for educators

Free limits: Only a few advanced searches and basic AI models — few or no pictures.

Education Pro ($10/mo for verified educators): Many more advanced searches, Research mode for thorough multi-source reports, image generation, and access to Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini all from one place — useful if you want to compare answers without juggling accounts.

💡 The honest take: Start with the free teacher plans — you may never need to pay. If you ever do, pay for just the one you use most.
